The exact region. Two paragraphs down, a claim about video hours gets a citation right under it: "NVIDIA GEAR's EgoScale pretrained a VLA on 20,854 hours..." followed by "Source: NVIDIA GEAR," and later "Ego4D... spans 3,670 hours..." followed by "Source: Ego4D." Scroll back up to the eight death numbers, 1 in 3 farm injuries, 108,000 construction deaths, 100,000 fishers, 860,000 from bad air, 110 per 100,000 in logging, and not one of them carries a source. Not a name, not a footnote, nothing.

What it made me feel, plainly. It made me trust the page less, not more. If you can be bothered to name your source for how many hours of warehouse video exist, but not for how many people die fishing every year, that tells me which numbers you checked twice and which ones you reached for because they sounded right.

Why a normal person reads it that way. People notice what gets sourced and what doesn't, even without being told to look for it. A number about dead workers, unsourced, sitting next to a number about footage hours that does get a named source, reads as the company caring more about being unimpeachable to a technical buyer than about being accurate on the human cost it's using to open its pitch. That is backwards from what would make me trust it.

The change as the shape of the words. Every death number gets the same treatment the two footage-hour numbers already get: a name next to it. If a number can't carry a source, it doesn't belong on the page at all.
