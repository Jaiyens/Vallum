What I see: in `artifacts/1440/home.png` (crop of y=3700 to 4900), the section heading "the work that kills." sits centered above the eight-photo grid, set in JetBrains Mono at a size that reads at roughly 32 to 36px, clearly larger than body text and functioning as the section's display line. The accessibility tree confirms this is a real level-2 heading: `heading "the work that kills, the work no one records, the work machines must learn" [level=2]` (`artifacts/1440/home.dom.txt` line 93).

Why it reads wrong: LOOK.md's type scale restricts JetBrains Mono to one job: "Data... Weight 400 only. Lowercase only. Never letter-spaced, never a label, never an eyebrow." A section's own opening heading, rendered large and centered, is squarely a display role, not a data annotation. The art-director's decision log makes the exact same call for the Helix's typed centerpiece line and generalizes the reasoning: "set the typed Helix centerpiece in the display serif rather than mono because mono display is the generated-design tell." This heading commits precisely that tell one section earlier than the typed line does. It also breaks the promised six-step scale (display-1, display-2, heading, body, small, data), since a heading-level element here is rendered in the data step at non-data size, a seventh, unspecified configuration.

The specific change: set "the work that kills." at the heading step (Newsreader 500, 26px/24px/22px) or display-2 if it is meant to carry beat 2's full weight, in forest or bone depending on the surface, not JetBrains Mono at display size.
