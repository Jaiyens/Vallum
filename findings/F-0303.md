The structural mismatch: Method's opening paragraph names the exact facts (a trilingual release, site-owner authorization, face blur) at the same level of specificity that Dataset's "Consent and provenance" list states them one section later, in the same order. This is not a gist-then-spec pattern, since Method is already as specific as Dataset is; it is the same three claims delivered twice in adjacent beats, with only "participants are paid" and the collection window and capture kit as Method's unique contribution.

What it costs: for a reader budgeting under two minutes, part of beat 4 earns no new ground before beat 5 repeats it almost word for word, spending reading time on confirmation rather than progression at exactly the point the argument should be building toward the offer.

What would fix it: let Method carry only what Dataset's list does not (the window, the capture kit) and let the consent facts appear once, in Dataset's list, where a buyer would forward them to counsel anyway.
