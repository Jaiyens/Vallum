The gap in the argument. The entire defensibility case rides on one sentence: "The environments are hostile, access runs on trust, and consent has to survive a buyer's legal review... That is why nobody collects it, and it is exactly what Vallum Labs collects." That clause is the whole moat. Nothing after it cashes the claim out. The site never names who is doing the collecting, never gives a background, a location history, a reason a farm owner in the Western Cape would let this specific person walk a crew through a harvest with cameras strapped to their heads. The footer says only "Vallum Labs Inc. Cape Town, South Africa." There is no founder identity anywhere in the document, no first-person account of how trust gets built, only the procedural consent stack (releases, blur, POPIA) that follows access, not the thing that produces it.

What a skeptical reader concludes. Trust-based access is the one advantage on this page that cannot be verified by inspecting a schema or a legal clause, the way the consent stack can be. Everything else about the moat is procedure, and procedure is copyable by any well-funded competitor who hires local counsel. Access is the part that supposedly is not copyable, and it is the part the site is least willing to substantiate. A reader who has seen five data-vendor pitches this quarter reads an unsupported access claim as the tell that it might not survive contact with a second farm.

What would close it. One paragraph, in the company's own voice, on why this access exists, without naming a single deal or site.
