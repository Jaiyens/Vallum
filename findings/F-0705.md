The exact sentence. "The environments are hostile, access runs on trust, and consent has to survive a buyer's legal review. That is why nobody collects it, and it is exactly what Vallum Labs collects."

What it made me feel, plainly. A little cold. It reads like the company is glad other people find this too hard or too ugly to go do, because that's what makes this worth money to them. "That is why nobody collects it" is said the way you'd say it about a gap in a market, not a gap in whether anyone's looking out for a fisherman off West Africa or a roofer in the heat.

Why a normal person reads it that way. Said out loud to a friend, this sentence is "nobody else wanted to go film people doing this dangerous work, so we do." That can be read two ways: brave, or opportunistic, and the page doesn't do anything to tip it toward brave. There's no sentence nearby about why the danger should matter to Vallum beyond it being a business advantage. Without that, "we go where nobody else will" reads as "we found the thing nobody else was willing to profit from."

The change as the shape of the words. Put one sentence next to this that says what the company owes back to the people whose danger makes the footage valuable, not just what the danger is worth to a buyer.
