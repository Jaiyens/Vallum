The gap in the argument. "Machines are learning this work by watching" grounds the market entirely in two academic citations: NVIDIA GEAR's EgoScale (a 54% pretraining gain) and Ego4D's indoor-heavy corpus. Both establish that egocentric video helps models and that the existing corpus skews indoor. Neither establishes that a lab has ever paid a third party, in a commercial transaction, for outdoor egocentric footage at anything like the price the page itself proposes ($15,000 to $40,000 per pilot). The argument moves from "this category of data improves models" straight to "so buy it from us" without a step showing the category gets purchased at all, as opposed to captured in-house or scraped from existing corpora.

What a skeptical reader concludes. A performance benchmark from a lab's own research paper is evidence the data is useful. It is not evidence of a functioning market with a purchasing motion, a budget line, or a buyer who has ever paid anyone for hours of this kind. Those are different claims, and the page only makes the first one. To a reader pattern-matching "real market versus thesis," a benchmark citation reads as the weaker of the two available kinds of evidence.

What would close it. One attributed sentence establishing that labs already pay third parties for real-world hours of this general kind, not just that pretraining on such hours helps.
