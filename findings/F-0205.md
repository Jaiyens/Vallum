The gap in the argument. The founder passage builds its authority in two sentences: first, a farming childhood in Fresno, California (artifacts/1440/home.dom.txt, line 107, "I grew up in a farming family outside Fresno, California"), then, in the very next paragraph, the claim that access to this specific operation runs through farm familiarity (line 109, "I came to Cape Town on July 16, 2026, for the Western Cape harvest. The access this work needs starts with someone who already knows a farm."). The sentence is placed to imply the first fact answers the second, but it does not: growing up on or near a farm outside Fresno is a US Central Valley, English-language, first-world-agribusiness upbringing, and the operation this passage is defending is trust with Afrikaans- and isiXhosa-speaking growers in the Western Cape of South Africa, a different continent, language set, land-tenure history, and farming culture. The copy never states what, if anything, bridges the two: no prior relationship, no local introduction, no time already spent in the region. It states the general principle ("access starts with someone who already knows a farm") and lets the reader assume Jay is that someone for this specific harvest, without saying so.

What a skeptical reader concludes. This is the exact sentence a pre-seed read is looking for, because it is where the moat is supposed to stop being asserted and start being shown. Instead it swaps one unproven claim (trust runs on access) for another unproven claim (a Fresno childhood produces Western Cape access), and the second is not obviously true the way the sentence's placement wants it to feel. A reader who notices the geography gap concludes the access claim is still an assertion, just now dressed in biography instead of stated as thesis.

What would close it. One sentence that actually bridges Fresno to the Western Cape: what in Jay's background, relationships, or preparation gives him standing with growers in this specific place, stated without naming a signed farm or operator.
