The exact region. Its own tile, next to "fishing" in general: "west africa fishery. In West Africa, about one in a hundred fishers dies every year." The company's own page says its first collection window is the Western Cape, South Africa, and that everything past agriculture is "stated direction, not current operations." West Africa isn't mentioned anywhere else on the page.

What it made me feel, plainly. Like a place got used for its statistic and nothing else. Every other tile in that row ties back to something the page actually claims to be doing or planning, farming, construction, logging. West Africa shows up once, only to supply a striking number, then never comes up again.

Why a normal person reads it that way. If a company has no stated work in a place, and that place's only appearance on the page is a fatality rate, it looks like the region was picked for how dramatic the number is, not because it means anything to what this company does. That reads as using someone else's danger for color.

The change as the shape of the words. Either connect the region to something the company is actually doing, or leave it out and let the categories that match real operations carry the point.
