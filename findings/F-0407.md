What I see: `artifacts/states/home-focus.png`, cropped tight on the centerpiece text (the tail end "...ls." of "the work that kills."), shows plain bone-colored mono characters sitting directly on top of an unmodified fishing-boat photograph. There is no visible blur, no darkened stripe, no full-width band of any kind behind the letters, edges of the photo's own detail (rope, boat hull, surf) run right up to and behind the glyphs with no separation. The type is legible here only because this particular patch of photo happens to be dark. Immediately to the right in the same frame is bright white surf and sand.

Why it reads wrong: this is Recipe A in LOOK.md, spelled out in detail: "A horizontal band spanning the full canvas width, height = typed text block + 24px padding top and bottom... backdrop-filter: blur(24px) saturate(50%)... background-color: rgba(12,11,9,0.35)," with an explicit acceptance test: "bone text on this band measures at least 4.5:1 contrast on extracted frames at every 30 degrees of ring rotation, 12 samples minimum." None of that machinery is present. Text legibility over moving media is the first thing my brief asks me to check, and the one frame available already shows the exact failure mode the recipe exists to prevent: readable now only by the luck of a dark patch behind it, with bright surf one panel-width away. I can't confirm this fails at all 12 rotation angles because the crawler's rotation captures did not actually reach the Helix (see the companion finding on that gap), but the missing recipe is confirmed directly from this frame and from the component itself, which sets `font-mono text-bone` with no background or blur classes at all.

The specific change: wrap the centerpiece in a full-canvas-width band with `backdrop-filter: blur(24px) saturate(50%)` and `background-color: rgba(12,11,9,0.35)`, height equal to the text block plus 24px top and bottom padding, exactly as Recipe A specifies, so contrast holds regardless of which panel is behind it.
