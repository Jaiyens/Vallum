The would-you-sign test. Say I own the farm. The page tells me a lot that's actually reassuring: a release signed before recording, in English, Afrikaans, and isiXhosa, site authorization, faces blurred before export, participants paid, POPIA compliance named specifically. That's not vague, that's a checklist I could hand to a lawyer, and it's the strongest part of the whole page.

What it made me feel, plainly. Reassured about the paperwork, and still unsettled about the point of the paperwork. Every one of those protections is about the footage: who consented to being filmed, whether their face is hidden, whether the release is in a language they read. None of them is about my crew's actual future, whether this footage speeds up the day a machine does their job instead of them, whether they get anything if it does, whether "consent-cleared" and "cared about" are the same thing here.

Why a normal person reads it that way. A farm owner isn't only asking "is this legal." They're asking "am I about to help end my own crew's work, and does anyone here think that's my business to know." The page answers the first question thoroughly and never touches the second.

The change as the shape of the words. One honest paragraph, addressed to the farm owner and the crew, not the buyer, about what happens after the pilot if the footage works, would close the gap the consent checklist can't close by itself.
