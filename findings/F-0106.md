What I saw: artifacts/375/dataset.png "Consent and provenance" section; artifacts/1440/dataset.dom.txt lines 24-30. The list states a release exists, in three languages, with site-owner authorization, face blur, a per-clip provenance record, "POPIA compliant, including Section 57 on biometric data export," and C2PA signing where supported. Nowhere on this page, or on the home page, does it state what the release actually permits: whether participants consented to commercial licensing for AI/ML training specifically, to international export/transfer, to sublicensing or resale, or to a perpetual/irrevocable grant. It documents that consent was collected, never its scope.

Why it costs the reply: this is the page built to be forwarded to counsel specifically so the consent chain can clear before signature. Counsel's first question is never "was a form signed," it's "what did the form grant, and does it cover what we intend to do with the footage." A page that proves a release exists but not its scope forces a request for the actual release template before any redline can even start, turning a fast yes into an open-ended stall.

What would fix it, in one sentence: state in one line what the release grants (commercial AI-training use, geographic scope, sublicensable or not, term) next to where it lists that the release exists.
