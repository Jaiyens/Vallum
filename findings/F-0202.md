The gap in the argument. The first substantive content block after the hero is the Helix: eight equally sized tiles, farming, construction, fishing, west africa fishery, heat, air, logging, "the pattern," each with its own image and its own cited fatality statistic. A reader forms an impression of a company already working across the outdoor economy. Several screens later, "Consent first, camera second" narrows that to one sentence: the actual operation is agriculture only, one province, one country, a single window from July 2026 to approximately January 2027. Construction, solar, rigs, wind, and ironwork are labeled "stated direction, not current operations." The retraction is honest and correctly worded, but it arrives after the page has already spent its widest, most visual section building the opposite impression.

What a skeptical reader concludes. The sequence reads as scope-then-scale-down rather than scale-then-scope. A reader who only skims the top of the page (the stated read pattern for this exact reader, under two minutes on a phone) walks away thinking the company operates in eight sectors. The one who reads to the bottom learns it is one sector, one place, six months. That gap between first impression and disclosed reality is the kind of thing a diligence call opens with, and it is avoidable, because the honest scope is already written into the copy, just placed after the section that implies otherwise.

What would close it. State the single current window before or inside the Helix, not four sections after it.
