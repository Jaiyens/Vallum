The path I tried: compared the closing block on / (components/sections/footer.tsx, rendered via SiteFooter at the bottom of app/page.tsx) against the closing block on /dataset (app/dataset/page.tsx lines 76-84), then confirmed the difference in the two routes' actual ARIA snapshots (artifacts/1440/home.dom.txt line 220 vs artifacts/1440/dataset.dom.txt line 84).

What happened: home's footer wraps its two return links in `<nav aria-label="Footer">`, giving assistive tech a landmark to jump to at the end of every page, plus the DormantAsk echo and the forest entity line above it. /dataset's closing block ships a DormantAsk paragraph, then a single bare `<Link href="/">Home</Link>` with no surrounding nav element at all, on the page's plain bone body background rather than the forest strip that closes home. The ARIA tree confirms it: home has `navigation "Footer": link "Home", link "Dataset"`; /dataset has only `link "Home"` sitting directly in main, no nav landmark, no second link.

Where the reader stops: a screen reader or keyboard user who has learned, from browsing /, that the site's two routes live inside a "Footer" landmark at the end of every page gets no such landmark on /dataset, the one route IA.md expects to be forwarded to counsel as a standalone page. Landmark-based navigation ("jump to footer") that works on / silently does nothing on /dataset. The single "Home" link still works, but the two routes' end-of-document structure and affordance are inconsistent with each other.

The fix: wrap /dataset's closing Home link in the same `<nav aria-label="Footer">` pattern SiteFooter uses (self-link to /dataset can stay omitted), so both routes present the same landmark shape to assistive tech.
