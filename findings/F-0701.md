The exact sentence. "This is the work humans were never meant to do." Followed two lines later by: "Machines learn this work by watching it done. We film the people who still do it, so the machines can learn." This is the first real thing the page tells me, before a single word about consent, pay, or safety.

What it made me feel, plainly. It made my stomach drop. Not because filming farm workers is wrong, but because of the order these two sentences come in and what they add up to. First you tell me my father's whole working life, or a friend's, was a thing that "was never meant" to happen, like it's an embarrassing accident of history. Then, in the very next breath, you tell me the reason you're filming these people at all is so a machine can take over. Nobody in that sentence gets asked if they want to be replaced. Nobody gets told what happens to them once the machine has learned enough.

Why a normal person reads it that way. "The people who still do it" is doing a lot of quiet work. It puts the workers on the losing side of a countdown clock, "still" implying "not for much longer." Put "never meant to do" next to "so the machines can learn" and there is no other honest way to read it: this is a company whose stated purpose, in its own opening words, is to watch people work so their jobs can end. There is no companion sentence anywhere near the top that says this is meant to make the job safer, or that the workers get a stake in what gets built from their labor. A reasonable person reads intent from what a company chooses to say first, and what this company chose to say first is that the work, and implicitly the worker, is obsolete.

The change as the shape of the words. The opening beat needs a sentence that puts the worker on the same side as the machine's benefit, not on the clock being run out on them, before it ever gets to "so the machines can learn." Purpose stated before replacement stated, in that order, every time.
