## The argument in one sentence

Machines will learn dangerous outdoor work by watching the people who do it now, nobody has filmed that work, so Vallum Labs films it with the consent of the people in it and licenses the hours to robotics and world-model teams.

## The beats

### Beat 1: The work
- **Job:** Orient the reader. Put them inside outdoor manual work and name what the company trades in.
- **Earns the next beat by:** showing one crew's work as real and physical, which opens the question of what this work is at full scale and what it costs. The buyer also learns within ten seconds that this is a data supplier, so they grant the page its two minutes.
- **Reader knows on arrival:** nothing.
- **Reader knows on exit:** outdoor manual work exists as a filmed, first-person subject, and this company deals in footage of it.
- **Surface:** ink.
- **Copy shape:** one display line, 8 words or fewer, concrete, about the work itself, no verdict on the workers, no death. One sub line, 14 words or fewer, stating what the company collects and licenses and to whom, with no temporal judgment on the people doing the work. The hero film and its crew and twin labels do the showing. Zero statistics in this beat.
- **Cut if:** cannot be cut; a site with no orientation has no reader. If the film is not legible at 375, the film is replaced, not the beat.

### Beat 2: What it costs
- **Job:** Make the stakes physical. The industries, the conditions, and the two verified numbers.
- **Earns the next beat by:** posing a problem it refuses to resolve. A company that sells data has just shown the reader danger, the question of what footage has to do with any of this is now open, and beat 3 exists to answer it.
- **Reader knows on arrival:** outdoor work is real and this company films it.
- **Reader knows on exit:** this work is among the most dangerous work there is, stated with two cited numbers and otherwise shown through conditions, never through a body count per panel.
- **Surface:** ink. Last dark beat on the page.
- **Copy shape:** display line of 10 words or fewer carrying cost without relish, no "kills" as a standalone morsel. Six to eight panel labels of 1 to 3 words naming industries and conditions. Each panel focus state holds 15 to 30 words describing the work and its conditions: heights, seas, heat, machinery, weather. Exactly two panels carry a fatality statistic, cited, worded exactly as FACTS.md words them: the NIOSH tractor-overturn figure on the agriculture panel and roofing at 48.7 per 100,000 on the rooftop panel. These are the only two fatality numbers on the entire site. Panel imagery follows the footage-provenance law: empty dangerous places, no stock workers.
- **Cut if:** the Helix cannot ship legible and scroll-safe on a phone. Then the beat survives as a static panel row with identical copy; the mechanism dies, not the beat.

### Beat 3: The turn
- **Job:** Connect machines to this work through watching, then reveal the hole: the watching has been done indoors, and the outdoor record is empty.
- **Earns the next beat by:** making the camera inevitable. If the missing input is outdoor first-person footage, someone has to go get it, and the only question left is how.
- **Reader knows on arrival:** the work is dangerous, and a data company showed them that for a reason not yet stated.
- **Reader knows on exit:** robots and world models learn physical work from egocentric video, proven at scale (EgoScale, cited); the reference corpora were shot in kitchens, warehouses, and homes (Ego4D, cited; the indoor-gap claim rests on the FACTS.md line that indoor-focused collectors do not cover this work); outdoor manual work is missing from the record because the environments are hostile, access runs on trust, and consent has to survive legal review; that is exactly what Vallum Labs collects. The reason a machine should do this work is the person doing it now.
- **Surface:** ink resolving to bone. The page turns light at the exact point the company's answer enters, and stays light.
- **Copy shape:** three short paragraphs, 70 to 100 words total. Paragraph one: the learning fact with the EgoScale numbers and source in the same sentence (the NVIDIA GEAR pattern: claim, number, source, one sentence). Paragraph two: where the existing footage came from, Ego4D cited. Paragraph three: what is missing and why, ending on the positive statement of what Vallum Labs collects. Positive statements only; no "unlike," no "we are not," no competitor named or gestured at.
- **Cut if:** the EgoScale and Ego4D facts leave the ledger. That condition is theoretical; this beat is load-bearing and the site has no argument without it.

### Beat 4: The method
- **Job:** Show how the footage gets made. Consent first, camera second; today's kit named honestly; the window.
- **Earns the next beat by:** a method that runs produces a product, so the reader's next question is what exactly ships and on what terms.
- **Reader knows on arrival:** the missing input is outdoor first-person footage and someone has to collect it.
- **Reader knows on exit:** every capture starts with a signed release in English, Afrikaans, and isiXhosa plus site-owner authorization; faces blur before export; the first window is the Western Cape harvest, July 2026 to approximately January 2027, agriculture first, with construction, rooftop solar, offshore rigs, wind turbines, and high-rise ironwork as stated direction, not current operations; the kit today is 4 to 6 refurbished iPhones on head straps carried in personally, head-mounted, 1080p minimum, 30fps; the rig render is a direction, not a product.
- **Surface:** bone.
- **Copy shape:** heading of 6 words or fewer. Body 80 to 110 words in three paragraphs: consent process, window and direction, kit. All claims drawn strictly from FACTS.md; the payment-to-participants sentence appears only if Jay adds it to the ledger. The rig exploded view closes this beat, framed by one plain today-and-tomorrow sentence in the page's voice (Figure's generational pattern: each generation has a stated job). The honest kit sentence is mandatory wherever the render appears. No status badges, no mono-caps qualifiers.
- **Cut if:** the rig render still reads as a product claim next to the honest sentence. Then the render is cut and the kit paragraph keeps the truth. The consent content is never cut.

### Beat 5: What ships
- **Job:** Make the dataset a purchasable thing: schema, format, rights, provenance, offer, ask.
- **Earns the next beat by:** a reader who just walked a rights stack this heavy asks why it is built this way, and beat 6 answers.
- **Reader knows on arrival:** the method exists and runs in a stated window.
- **Reader knows on exit:** all three buyer questions are answered. What am I buying and in what format: consent-cleared, action-labeled egocentric video, annotated to conventions compatible with Ego4D and Ego-Exo4D, the eight schema fields. Is the rights story clean: the six-item consent and provenance stack, POPIA Section 57 included. Will this person deliver: exact pilot terms, $15,000 to $40,000 for 20 to 40 curated hours, 50% on signature, 50% on delivery and acceptance, non-exclusive by default, exclusivity time-boxed and task-boxed as a paid premium. The reader also knows how to act: one contact action lives at the end of this beat. Depth does the positioning: no robotics company documents a consent chain, so no denial is ever needed.
- **Surface:** bone.
- **Copy shape:** intro of 35 to 50 words. Annotation schema as the 8-item FACTS.md list, consent and provenance as the 6-item list, rules not cards, hairlines not boxes. The offer as one 45 to 60 word paragraph with exact terms. Then the ask: a single contact action, label of 2 to 4 words that says what happens when pressed (the old-money pattern: labels name the job). This is the only full ask on the page; the footer may echo it in one line.
- **Cut if:** never while the site sells anything; this beat is the store. If offer terms leave the ledger, the offer paragraph is rewritten to schema and rights only and the beat stands.

### Beat 6: Why it is done this way
- **Job:** The ethos, plainly. The workers are the source of the data and never the subject; the stack exists because of them.
- **Earns the next beat by:** closing the argument. Nothing remains to say but the name.
- **Reader knows on arrival:** the buyer's questions are answered. The civilian reader's question, whether this company is ghoulish, is still open.
- **Reader knows on exit:** consent is signed before recording in the participant's own language, faces are blurred before anything leaves the pipeline, provenance ties every clip to a signed release, POPIA Section 57 governs biometric export, and the reason a machine should do this work is the person doing it now, said once, plainly, and then stopped.
- **Surface:** forest, full bleed. The one dark moment of the ending: matte, calm, alive, not grim.
- **Copy shape:** 60 to 90 words, first-person company voice, procedures not adjectives (the Patagonia and Kickstarter pattern: a trust move you can check beats a label you must believe). Items drawn strictly from the FACTS.md consent stack. No fatality numbers; that budget was spent in beat 2. No benefit-corporation vocabulary in any form. The payment claim appears only if it enters the ledger.
- **Cut if:** it cannot be written without a body count or a label. Then it collapses to one plain paragraph at the tail of beat 5 rather than a full section.

## The seams

- 1 to 2: the hero shows one crew doing the work; the Helix widens to the whole outdoor economy and prices it. The reader continues because the hero made a world and the Helix says what living in it costs.
- 2 to 3: beat 2 states the cost and offers no resolution. The only reason a data company shows you danger is that data is the fix, and beat 3 says how, which converts an open discomfort into an argument.
- 3 to 4: an empty record makes a camera necessary; beat 4 makes it legitimate. Consent first, camera second means the camera arrives already answered for instead of from nowhere. This is the seam that was broken and this ordering is the repair.
- 4 to 5: a running method implies a product; the buyer's format and rights questions come due and beat 5 pays them in full.
- 5 to 6: the weight of the stack raises the question why; the ethos is the reason the stack exists, so beat 6 explains beat 5 instead of decorating it.
- 6 to 7: the argument is closed; the name signs it, in light.

## What I cut and why

- The hero line "This is the work humans were never meant to do." Cut. It passes a verdict on the workers that contradicts the ethos (the reason a robot should do this job is the person who currently does it) and it carries no attributable fact.
- Six of the eight Helix fatality statistics (farming one-in-three, construction 108,000, fishing 100,000, West Africa one-in-a-hundred, heat 2.4 billion exposed, air 860,000, and the 63% pattern panel). Cut. FACTS.md caps fatality statistics at two site-wide and contains none of these numbers; eight body counts in one section is also the Palantir failure regardless of sourcing.
- "The pattern" as a ninth panel. Cut with its statistic; a summary panel exists only to say the number again.
- "No one records this work" from the Helix heading. Moved to beat 3. The current three-part heading spends the turn inside beat 2 and leaves beat 3 nothing to reveal.
- The standalone rig section after the offer. Cut as a beat, folded into beat 4. Hardware is method; placed after the offer it read as a pivot to being a hardware company, which is the exact confusion the no-negation rule exists to prevent.
- The Egocentric-1M million-hours fact. Cut from beat 3. It cannot be attributed without naming a competitor, and the brief requires the gap made obvious without naming one. EgoScale plus Ego4D carry the turn on their own.
- The founder letter (present in an earlier build, absent from the crawl). Not restored. The ethos beat carries the founder's convictions in the company's first-person voice; a signed letter after the offer reads as investor material and the site does not sell to investors.
